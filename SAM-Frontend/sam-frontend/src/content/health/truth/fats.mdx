“Fat” is often misunderstood. Many think all fats are harmful, but the reality is more nuanced. Our bodies need certain fats for health — but the type and source of fat matter greatly. Science now confirms what the Bible and the Spirit of Prophecy have long taught about the best fats for our health.

## 1. What Science Says About Fats

Modern research makes a strong distinction between different types of fats and their health impacts.

<Quote
  category="science"
  source="NIH-AARP / JAMA Internal Medicine"
  reference="large cohort study"
>
  Higher intake of plant-based fats (from grains and vegetable oils) was associated with lower overall and cardiovascular mortality, while animal-based fats (from dairy and eggs) were linked to higher mortality.
</Quote>

## 2. Plant vs Animal Fats: Why Source Matters

Not all fats are created equal. The source of the fat — whether plant or animal — plays a critical role in its effect on our health.

<Quote
  category="science"
  source="Harvard Research"
  reference="cardiovascular studies"
>
  When saturated fats or trans fats are replaced by plant-based monounsaturated fats, heart disease risk drops.
</Quote>

<Quote
  category="science"
  source="Animal Studies"
  reference="atherosclerosis experiments"
>
  Animals fed plant fats showed less atherosclerosis than those fed animal fats.
</Quote>

## 3. Why Some “Healthy” Fats Can Be Misleading

Some plant-based diets are mistakenly assumed to be “low fat,” but true plant-based diets may include high-fat plant foods (like nuts and seeds) — and that’s not necessarily bad.

<Quote
  category="science"
  source="Nutritional Epidemiology"
  reference="dietary fat composition"
>
  In plant-based diets, unsaturated fatty acids predominate; saturated fats are more common in animal foods like dairy and eggs, which are linked with higher cardiovascular risk.
</Quote>

It’s not just about reducing fat — it’s about choosing the right fats.

## 6. Health Benefits of the Right Fats

Choosing healthy fats — especially from plant sources — offers many advantages:

- Reduced risk of heart disease and cardiovascular death  
- Improved cholesterol profile when saturated fat is replaced by unsaturated plant-based fats  
- Anti-inflammatory benefits from unsaturated fats found in nuts, seeds, and vegetable oils  
- Better long-term health outcomes when sources of fat are carefully chosen  

## 7. Practical Tips: How to Choose Healthy Fats

Here are some practical, faith-aligned ways to incorporate healthy fats into your diet:

- Use olive oil or other plant oils for cooking and dressings  
- Include nuts and seeds (e.g., walnuts, flax, chia) as sources of healthy unsaturated fats  
- Limit or avoid animal fats such as butter, lard, and high-fat dairy  
- Replace 5% of calories from animal fats with plant-based fats to reduce cardiovascular risk  
- Avoid fried, greasy foods and heavy animal-based dishes; practice moderation and simplicity in eating  

The truth about fats is that not all fats are bad — but the source matters greatly. God’s design, backed by prophetic counsel, points us to plant-based, easily digestible fats like olive oil and nuts, and away from greasy, heavy animal fats. Science echoes this wisdom: choosing the right fats supports heart health, reduces disease risk, and promotes long-term vitality.  

**When we choose God’s health plan for fats, we care for His temple in body and spirit.**
